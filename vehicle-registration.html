Vehicle Registration

Vehicle Registration is required for all vehicles operated on public roads. Registering your vehicle links the vehicle to its owner and ensures it is properly recorded with the motor vehicle agency. During the registration process, the vehicle owner provides identification, proof of insurance, ownership documentation, and payment of applicable taxes and fees.

Before beginning the registration process, make sure you have the required documents available, including proof of insurance, a valid driver license or identification, and the vehicle title or bill of sale.

Registering a New Vehicle and Common Questions

When purchasing a vehicle from a licensed dealership, the dealership will typically handle most of the required paperwork on your behalf. This often includes submitting the vehicle title transfer, registration application, and any applicable taxes and fees to the motor vehicle authority. In many cases, the dealership will provide temporary registration or license plates so the vehicle can be legally driven while the official documents are processed.

If you purchase a used vehicle from a private seller, auction, or other non-dealership source, you are generally responsible for completing the vehicle registration and title transfer yourself. To do this, you must visit or schedule an appointment with your local motor vehicle agency or licensing office. During this process, you may be required to provide several documents, such as:
- The signed vehicle title from the previous owner
- A bill of sale or proof of purchase
- Valid identification
- Proof of insurance coverage
- Payment for applicable taxes, title fees, and registration fees

If the vehicle was purchased without financing, the seller or dealership may provide you with the original title and supporting paperwork so that you can complete the title transfer and registration yourself. If the vehicle is financed, the lending institution may hold the title until the loan has been paid in full.

Vehicles brought in from another region or jurisdiction may require additional steps before registration can be completed. These steps may include a vehicle identification number (VIN) verification, emissions inspection, or safety inspection depending on local regulations.
Vehicle Registration Renewal

- Registration Renewal Notice or Vehicle Identification Number
- Your insurance card or company name and policy number
- License plate number
- Proof of ID (Driver license or other primary ID document. See 6 Points of ID for more information).
- Power of Attorney if renewing a registration for someone else
- The amount due indicated in your renewal notice. You can pay by cash, money order, checks (payable to NJMVC), credit or debit card
- Completed Application for Vehicle Registration (Form BA-49)
- Schedule an Appointment

To renew your registration by mail, you will need to complete the Application for Vehicle Registration (Form BA-49) and submit it along with the required documents and payment to the DMV.
